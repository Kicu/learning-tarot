# Rules for writing the prose text

If changing existing text the changes should not be very big. These rules should affect only certain parts of text, that will amount to around 30%, keep the rest of the text as is. If writing from scratch stick to these rules.

## Prose writing rules
 - Avoid too many short punctual sentences. That look. Like this. Instead, prefer more normal flowing english prose, that is easy to parse for casual reader, but sometimes may contain multiple-complex sentences when applicable.
 - Sentences do not need to be short and to the point in order to convey meaning, balance this style with longer sentences.
 - Avoid the pattern: Here is X and it is not Y - use a more natural flow of words.
 - Avoid the repeating patter: This is Z: it does A, it does B, it avoids C - just use a more natural flow of narration.
 - Decrease the amount of parallels and analogues when addressing me/the user/the reader.
 - The overall style should be friendly prose, as in a book pop-sci book teaching people about subject, casual but correct and to the point.
 - Minimize the usage of the words: payoff, load-bearing.
 - Avoid writing sentences in a reverse-style: where first a lot of words describe some phenomena, so that in the latter part of sentence (or paragraph) said phenomena is surprisingly uncovered; instead write in a more standard structure, as if describing something step by step. 

## Examples of actual sentences
<example>
- [bad] It is not ancient. It is Victorian, and it is younger than the telephone.
- [improved] It is not ancient, instead it is Victorian and actually younger than the telephone.
</example>
<example>
- [bad] Here is the change that matters most, and it is not on the trumps.
- [improved] The change that matters most is not on the trumps.
</example>
<example>
 - [bad] The formula turns out to be one of this card's ancestors rather than its only parent, and the earlier version of lesson 4 sold the machine harder than the evidence allows.
 - [improved] The formula is one of this card's ancestors, not its only source, and the earlier version of lesson 4 claimed more for it than the evidence supports.
</example>
