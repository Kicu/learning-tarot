The Twenty-Two Trumps

The trump sequence at a glance: each card's 1450 identity, its Golden Dawn overlay, Waite's printed meaning, and the visual evidence to point at. Rows I–XI are compressed from lesson 8, rows XII–XXI and the Fool from lesson 9. The second table is the drill sheet: one spoken line per trump.

How to read a trump

StepAskSay
11450 identityWhat did a fifteenth-century player see — a station, a virtue, a force, a scene from the Last Things? Where it stands in the sequence tells you which stretch it belongs to (table below).
2The overlayWhat the occultists renamed or re-dressed, and what Waite and Smith changed in 1909 — always with the date attached.
3PictureThe detail that carries the meaning. The picture wins any conflict with a printed list — Waite's own Hermit and Star entries are the proof cases.
—RegisterA trump raises the stakes of the whole question (lesson 2): this is about the shape of things, not one afternoon of them.

The shape of the sequence

StretchWhat it holds
I–VPeople, by station: conjuror → Church → Empress → Emperor → Pope. The game's ranking restates the medieval order of precedence.
VI–VIIWhat life deals the people: love arrives (a choice, before Waite made it Eden), victory gets its parade.
VIII–XIWhat outranks a victor: Fortitude, Time, Fortune, Justice — character, age, luck and law.
XII–XIIIHow a life ends: in disgrace (the Traitor) or in the ordinary way (Death, who takes every rank).
XIV–XVIBetween the worlds: the last virtue, then Hell and God's fire — the Devil and the struck Tower. Tarot Heritage calls XV–XXI the Apocalypse sequence.
XVII–XIXThe lights, in order of brightness: Star, Moon, Sun — a full moon outshines the stars, the sun outshines both, as higher trumps beat lower.
XX–XXIThe end of time: the Angel's trumpet, then the renewed world.
0Outside the ranks: the Fool, never a trump — see below for the three places he has been put.

The order in your hands is the French one, settled by the printed decks. Fifteenth-century Italy varied it: some decks grouped the three virtues together after the Pope, and in Florence and Bologna the World came before the Angel rather than after (Tarot Heritage, on the World).

The Strength–Justice swap

Marseille decks number Justice VIII and Fortitude XI; the RWS swaps them. Waite in print: "For reasons which satisfy myself, this card has been interchanged with that of Justice… there is no cause for explanation" (Pictorial Key, VIII). The unstated reason is Book T's table: with the Hebrew letters in alphabet order, the zodiac signs only run in sequence if the lion card holds the Leo slot at eight and the scales the Libra slot at eleven. A deck numbered the old way is declining Waite's edit, not making an error.

Where the Fool goes

PlacementWho, whenStatus
Outside the sequenceThe game, 1440s onward: the Fool is the "excuse", played instead of following suit; he wins no trick and holds no rank.Documented
Between XX and XXIThe French occult order (Lévi's letter Shin). Waite's book keeps it while disowning it: "the Zero therefore appears after No. 20… Wherever it ought to be put, the Zero is an unnumbered card" (Pictorial Key, Part II).Invented, 1850s; documented as Waite's printed order
First, as 0The Golden Dawn's Book T gives the Fool the first letter, Aleph, and the element air; the modern deck order (0 before I) and the "Fool's Journey" reading (Eden Gray, 1970) follow from it.Invented, c. 1888

At the table · the drill rows

One spoken line per trump, tuned to Smith's picture and checked against today's mainstream. This is the text the flashcard drill in lesson 9 grades against; the lesson's JSON deck duplicates these cells verbatim, so an edit here must be made there too.

TrumpThe sceneAt the table
0 FoolA young man in bright clothes steps toward a cliff edge, a bundle on his stick and a small white dog bounding at his heels; the sun is behind him.A beginning taken on trust — the step before the plan, innocence with the drop in full view.
I MagicianA robed figure at a table laid with the four suit emblems raises a wand skyward and points to the earth; a lemniscate floats over his head.Skill and will put to work — the tools are on the table and the act begins.
II High PriestessA veiled woman sits between a black and a white pillar with a half-covered scroll in her lap and a crescent at her feet.What is not yet being told — secrets kept, intuition, the answer still behind the veil.
III EmpressA crowned woman reclines on cushions in a wheat field, a heart-shaped shield with the Venus sign beside her.Fruitfulness — abundance and nurture, things growing because they are tended.
IV EmperorAn armoured man sits on a bare stone throne carved with rams' heads, sceptre in hand.Authority and structure — the rule that holds, protection by order.
V HierophantA pope enthroned between two pillars blesses two tonsured monks; crossed keys lie at his feet.Tradition and institutions — the established channel, doing it the proper way.
VI LoversA nude man and woman stand beneath a great angel, the serpent's tree behind her and a flaming tree behind him.Love and union — attraction with a choice inside it, values brought into line.
VII ChariotA crowned figure stands in a canopied car drawn by a black and a white sphinx, a walled city behind him.Victory by control — opposed forces driven forward by will; watch for presumption.
VIII StrengthA woman gently closes a lion's jaws, a lemniscate over her head and a chain of flowers around them both.Fortitude — courage held calmly, the beast mastered by patience rather than force.
IX HermitAn old cloaked man stands alone on a grey summit, holding out a lantern with a star inside it.Withdrawal to seek — solitude, guidance dearly bought, the light carried for those behind.
X Wheel of FortuneA wheel lettered TARO turns, a sphinx steady on top, a serpent sliding down and a jackal-headed figure rising; four winged creatures read in the corners.The turn of circumstances — luck, a cycle moving, what nobody at the table controls.
XI JusticeA crowned figure enthroned between pillars holds an upright sword and level scales.Fairness and consequence — the deserving side, cause meeting effect, a decision that binds.
XII Hanged ManA man hangs by one foot from a living T-shaped tree, hands behind his back, face calm, a halo about his head.Suspension — the pause, surrender, the view from the other way up.
XIII DeathAn armoured skeleton rides a white horse under a black banner with a white rose; a king lies fallen and a bishop pleads; the sun rises between two towers.An ending that clears the ground — the close of something, with the next thing already dawning.
XIV TemperanceA winged angel pours water between two cups, one foot in a pool and one on land; a path leads to a lit crown on the horizon.Moderation and blending — balance kept by careful mixing, patience, a steady hand.
XV DevilA horned, bat-winged figure squats on a pedestal with an inverted torch; a chained man and woman with tails stand below him.Bondage chosen — attachment, compulsion, the chain that could be lifted and is not.
XVI TowerLightning knocks the crown from a tower on a crag; flames burst from the windows and two figures fall.Sudden collapse — a false structure comes down all at once; upheaval, then clear air.
XVII StarA naked woman kneels at a pool pouring from two jugs, one onto the land and one into the water, under a great eight-rayed star and seven smaller ones.Hope — renewal after the storm, calm restored, something given freely.
XVIII MoonA moon in profile looks down on a dog and a wolf howling; a crayfish crawls from a pool; a path runs between two towers into the distance.Uncertainty by half-light — fear, illusion, the imagination working without a clear view.
XIX SunA naked child rides a white horse carrying a red banner past a wall of sunflowers under a full-faced sun.Joy in the open — success, warmth, vitality, nothing left hidden.
XX JudgementAn angel blows a trumpet hung with a cross banner; below, grey figures rise from coffins on the sea with arms raised.The call — reckoning and renewal, a summons answered from inside, absolution.
XXI WorldA dancer wrapped in a purple sash floats inside a laurel wreath, a wand in each hand; the four creatures watch from the corners.Completion — the whole thing done and integrated, the journey ended, the world open.

Wording note: Book T survives in copies that vary. The mr-kaplan mirror prints the High Priestess's title as "The Priestess the Silver Star"; other copies read "The Priestess of the Silver Star," which this sheet uses.
