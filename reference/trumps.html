The Twenty-Two Trumps

This sheet covers each trump's meaning around 1450, the Golden Dawn's later additions, Waite's published interpretation, and picture details you can use in a reading. Rows I–XI summarise lesson 8, while rows XII–XXI and the Fool summarise lesson 9. The final table gives a short meaning to practise for each card.

How to read a trump

StepAskSay
1Original subjectIdentify the card's original subject, such as a ruler, courage, or death, and look for shared themes with neighbouring cards.
2Later changesDescribe the names, symbols, or meanings added by later occult writers, including Waite and Smith's changes in 1909, and give the dates.
3PicturePoint to a detail in the picture that supports your interpretation. For this course, use the picture when it conflicts with Waite's list of meanings; see the Hermit and Star below for examples.
—ImportanceIn a reading, a trump suggests a major theme in the situation, such as a change in direction, a recurring problem, or an important decision (lesson 2).

Groups of cards in the sequence

Card numbersShared theme
I–VThese cards move up through medieval social ranks, from the street performer to the Popess (representing the Church), Empress, Emperor, and Pope.
VI–VIIThe Lovers shows a choice in love in earlier decks and Adam and Eve in Waite's version, while the Chariot shows a victory parade.
VIII–XIStrength, the Hermit, the Wheel, and Justice represent courage, time, luck, and justice. They rank above the Chariot because even a victorious person remains subject to these influences.
XII–XIIIThe Hanged Man depicts a traitor punished with disgrace, while Death affects everyone, regardless of social rank.
XIV–XVITemperance represents moderation, followed by the Devil and the Tower's scene of divine punishment. Tarot Heritage groups XV–XXI as scenes about the end of the world.
XVII–XIXThe Star, Moon, and Sun increase in brightness as their rank increases in the card game.
XX–XXIJudgement shows the dead rising at an angel's trumpet call, followed by the World, representing a renewed world.
0The Fool has no trump rank in the original game. Later occult traditions place it at different points in the sequence, as explained below.

The sequence shown here follows the French order established through printed decks, with Strength and Justice exchanged in the Rider–Waite–Smith deck (see below). Card order varied in fifteenth-century Italy: some decks grouped the three virtues together after the Pope, and in Florence and Bologna the World came before the Angel rather than after (Tarot Heritage, on the World).

Game rank and symbolic meaning

A higher number gives a trump higher game rank, not necessarily greater spirituality. The Pope is a human office; Love and the Chariot still concern human life. Waite says the Chariot's rider cannot understand the High Priestess's mysteries, despite VII following II (Part II, VII). Explanations of every neighbouring pair require separate evidence; a coherent story alone does not establish the makers' intentions. See lesson 12.

The Strength–Justice swap

Marseille decks number Justice VIII and Strength (Fortitude) XI, while the Rider–Waite–Smith deck reverses them. Waite writes: "For reasons which satisfy myself, this card has been interchanged with that of Justice… there is no cause for explanation" (Pictorial Key, VIII). Book T's table explains the connection: when the cards follow the order of the Hebrew alphabet, putting Strength (the lion) at VIII and Justice (the scales) at XI also puts their zodiac signs, Leo and Libra, in the correct order. Both numbering systems are valid.

Where the Fool goes

PlacementWho, whenStatus
Outside the sequenceIn the game from the 1440s onward, the Fool is the "excuse": you can play it instead of a card of the required suit, but it cannot win the round and has no rank.Documented
Between XX and XXILévi's French occult system associates the Fool with the Hebrew letter Shin and places it here. Waite's book uses this position, though he describes it as uncertain: "the Zero therefore appears after No. 20… Wherever it ought to be put, the Zero is an unnumbered card" (Pictorial Key, Part II).Invented, 1850s; documented as Waite's printed order
First, as 0The Golden Dawn's Book T assigns the Fool the first Hebrew letter, Aleph, and the element air. This places 0 before I, as in modern decks and Eden Gray's "Fool's Journey" interpretation (1970).Invented, c. 1888

Practice: what to say during a reading

These short meanings are based on Smith's pictures and compared with common modern interpretations. The flashcard exercise in lesson 9 uses these same descriptions and meanings. Practise explaining the meaning in your own words.

TrumpThe sceneAt the table
0 FoolA young man in bright clothes steps toward a cliff edge, a bundle on his stick and a small white dog bounding at his heels; the sun is behind him.Starting something new with trust and curiosity, before you have a full plan or understand all the risks.
I MagicianA robed figure at a table laid with the four suit emblems raises a wand skyward and points to the earth; an infinity symbol floats over his head.Using your skills and the resources you already have to take action.
II High PriestessA veiled woman sits between a black and a white pillar with a half-covered scroll in her lap and a crescent at her feet.Some information is still hidden; pay attention to your intuition while you wait to learn more.
III EmpressA crowned woman reclines on cushions in a wheat field, a heart-shaped shield with the Venus sign beside her.Growth and abundance through care, such as supporting a person, a relationship, or a project.
IV EmperorAn armoured man sits on a bare stone throne carved with rams' heads, sceptre in hand.Creating stability and protection through clear rules, responsibility, and leadership.
V HierophantA pope enthroned between two pillars blesses two monks with shaved crowns; crossed keys lie at his feet.Following established customs or seeking guidance from a teacher, religion, or institution.
VI LoversA nude man and woman stand beneath a great angel, the serpent's tree behind her and a flaming tree behind him.Love and partnership, with choices about whether your values and commitments fit together.
VII ChariotA crowned figure stands under a chariot's canopy, with a black and a white sphinx in front and a walled city behind him.Making progress through determination and keeping competing aims under control, while avoiding overconfidence.
VIII StrengthA woman gently closes a lion's jaws, an infinity symbol over her head and a chain of flowers around them both.Facing difficulty with courage, patience, and gentle self-control.
IX HermitAn old cloaked man stands alone on a grey summit, holding out a lantern with a star inside it.Taking time alone to reflect, learn from experience, and find guidance you can also share with others.
X Wheel of FortuneA wheel lettered TARO turns, a sphinx steady on top, a serpent sliding down and a jackal-headed figure rising; four winged creatures read in the corners.Circumstances are changing, with luck and repeating patterns that you cannot fully control.
XI JusticeA crowned figure enthroned between pillars holds an upright sword and level scales.Making a fair decision, taking responsibility for your actions, and accepting their consequences.
XII Hanged ManA man hangs by one foot from a living T-shaped tree, hands behind his back, face calm, a halo about his head.Pausing, letting go of control, and looking at the situation from a different point of view.
XIII DeathAn armoured skeleton rides a white horse under a black banner with a white rose; a king lies fallen and a bishop pleads; the sun rises between two towers.Something is ending, such as a habit, relationship, or phase of life, making room for change.
XIV TemperanceA winged angel pours water between two cups, one foot in a pool and one on land; a path leads to a lit crown on the horizon.Finding balance by combining different needs or approaches patiently and avoiding extremes.
XV DevilA horned, bat-winged figure squats on a pedestal with an inverted torch; a chained man and woman with tails stand below him.Feeling trapped by an attachment or a repeated urge, and recognising where change may be possible.
XVI TowerLightning knocks the crown from a tower on a crag; flames burst from the windows and two figures fall.A sudden disruption exposes a weakness in a belief, plan, or situation and forces you to reassess it.
XVII StarA naked woman kneels at a pool pouring from two jugs, one onto the land and one into the water, under a great eight-rayed star and seven smaller ones.Regaining hope and calm after difficulty, with a sense of renewal and generosity.
XVIII MoonA moon in profile looks down on a dog and a wolf howling; a crayfish crawls from a pool; a path runs between two towers into the distance.Feeling uncertain because you cannot see the situation clearly, and fears or imagination may mislead you.
XIX SunA naked child rides a white horse carrying a red banner past a wall of sunflowers under a full-faced sun.Feeling joyful, energetic, and confident, with success and a clearer understanding of the situation.
XX JudgementAn angel blows a trumpet hung with a cross banner; below, grey figures rise from coffins on the sea with arms raised.Reviewing your past actions, accepting what needs to change, and finding forgiveness or a fresh start.
XXI WorldA dancer wrapped in a purple sash floats inside a laurel wreath, a wand in each hand; the four creatures watch from the corners.Completing an important stage, bringing together what you have learned, and being ready for what comes next.
