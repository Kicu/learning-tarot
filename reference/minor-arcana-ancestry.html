Where a Minor Card's Meaning Comes From

The streams that feed a number card's meaning, the dates they entered the deck, and the reading method that follows from taking them seriously. Compressed from lesson 4 and lesson 5.

How to read a number card

StepLook atSay
1SuitWhich of the four conversations this is — will, feeling, mind or matter (lesson 3).
2NumberWhich stage of the arc, and so what the stakes are — seed, crisis, recovery, completion (lesson 4).
3PictureThe plot: one literal sentence about what is happening in the scene, as if pausing a film.
—On conflictThe picture wins; the number sets the stakes. The scene is what the deck publishes and what you can point to; the stage says how secure the outcome is.

The streams, dated — with the Seven of Swords as the running sample

DateStreamWhat it contributedIts Seven of Swords
1780s Etteilla's cartomancy (Paris) The first systematic pip meanings — plain fortune-telling word-lists, no Kabbalah. Transmitted by Mathers' 1888 pamphlet ("chiefly taken from Etteilla") and still supplying roughly half of Waite's printed meanings (Revak's count: 49% strong matches). "Hope, Confidence, Desire, Attempt, Wish"
c. 1888 Book T (Golden Dawn) Titles and stage-structure from number × suit × decan — the arc of lesson 4. Strong on the fives, sixes and tens; scattered where the decans pull a row apart. Lord of Unstable Effort — "partial success, yielding when victory is within grasp"; spying and betrayed confidences in the small print
1909 Smith's pictures The scenes themselves: some staged from Book T titles, several composed after the Sola Busca deck (engraved c. 1491; photographs at the British Museum from October 1907), the rest her own theatrical invention. Waite's card-by-card coaching is documented only for trumps; no card-level briefs for the minors are recorded. A man stealing away from a camp, five swords carried, two left standing — after Sola Busca's sword-carrier
1911 Waite's Pictorial Key The companion book's divinatory lists — largely the Etteilla stream restated, occasionally contradicting the picture printed above them. "Design, attempt, wish, hope, confidence; also quarrelling, a plan that may fail"
1970 → The readers (Gray to keyword sites) Eden Gray's guides spliced all streams and read the scenes literally; mass practice then kept whichever meaning the picture supports. "An unwise attempt to make away with what is not yours" (Gray) → betrayal, deception, getting away with something (today)

Keep it labelled

"Diverged" and "aligned" measure agreement between two invented layers — 1888 doctrine and post-1909 scene-reading — not correctness. No stream is the true meaning; each is an assertion with a date, and the reading method above simply says which assertion to trust when they quarrel at the table.
