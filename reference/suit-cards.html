The Fifty-Six Suit Cards

Every suit card on one sheet — the 1888 title, the scene Smith drew, and the meaning to say at the table. The lookup this course's rules were building toward; the history behind the columns lives in the ancestry sheet, and the drill that moves the rows into memory is lesson 8.

After a draw, find the row and check yourself against the last two columns. The picture column is one literal sentence about what Smith painted — what you can point at. The at the table column is the meaning as this course says it aloud, tuned to the picture and cross-checked against today's mainstream. The title column is the memory glue: where it matches the scene it makes the row easy to keep, and where it quarrels with the scene, the picture wins — the standing rule from lesson 5.

🏆 Cups · feeling

Pagelearning itA fish looks back at the page from his cup, and he seems to be in conversation with it.Imagination stirring — an intuitive nudge, a small unexpected message.
Knightpursuing itA knight rides at a walk, cup carried level; Waite: "graceful, but not warlike".An offer approaching — romance or invitation, brought courteously.
Queenkeeping itOn a throne at the sea's edge she studies an ornate lidded cup, the only one closed in the deck.Feeling lived and tended — care, deep intuition, activity that feeds the dream.
Kingrunning itHis throne floats on the sea; the cup is held level while ships pitch behind him.Feeling governed, not indulged — calm counsel in weather that moves others.

🪙 Pentacles · matter

Kingrunning itHis throne and robe are grown over with vines; his castle stands behind him.Established wealth — steady management, the estate in good hands.

Documented

The title column is checkable against Book T (c. 1888–96), the aces' "Root of the Powers" names included; the stage words are the Sub Spe manuscript's, via Mary K. Greer. Waite's own card entries and the quoted court phrases are in the Pictorial Key (1911).

Keep it labelled

The "at the table" column is no one's doctrine. It is where a century of reading Smith's scenes literally has landed — Eden Gray's 1970 guide is the hinge, and today's mainstream (Biddy Tarot is the barometer this sheet was checked against) keeps whichever meaning the picture supports. Where a title and its scene quarrel — the audit is in the ancestry sheet — the picture wins.
