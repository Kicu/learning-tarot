# The number×suit formula is one ancestor of a minor card's meaning, not its generator — the Seven of Swords test

Mateusz ran the honest experiment lesson 4 invited: draw a number card blind, predict from chapter table + suit, then look. On the Seven of Swords it failed — "a possible result in the suit of mind and conflict" does not produce a man tiptoeing out of a camp with five stolen swords — and he correctly generalised the failure: the Tree-of-Life story is too abstract to generate Smith's very specific scenes, and the chapter names felt "dumbfounding" when combined with actual pictures.

Research confirmed his critique is the scholarly consensus. The RWS minors have at least three independent ancestries: (1) Etteilla's 1780s cartomantic word-lists, transmitted via Mathers 1888 — Revak's quantified comparison shows 49% of Waite's printed meanings strongly match the Etteilla school, and Waite's Seven of Swords list copies Mathers nearly verbatim; (2) Book T's number×suit×decan formula — which supplies stage and mood structure but shares no phrase with Waite's printed meaning for this card; (3) Smith's images, some borrowed from the Sola Busca deck (c. 1491) — including the Seven of Swords thief composition itself. Modern meanings (Eden Gray 1970 → Biddy) read the scenes literally, which is why "theft/deception" is today's meaning.

## Evidence

He designed the falsification test himself (blind draw + guess), quoted the lesson's gloss verbatim, and identified the exact card where formula and image diverge most — the card ranked #1 on the divergence list compiled from independent sources. He also flagged the teacher's overconfidence directly ("I know you think this all makes sense").

## Implications

- Correction adopted across the course: **stage from the number, plot from the picture; when they disagree, the picture wins** and the formula sets the stakes. The arc of [[0001-number-fixes-stage-not-mood]] survives as a base-rate scaffold (five→six turn, tens' completion), consistent with [[0003-suit-valence-is-a-base-rate]] — but it must never be sold as deriving scenes.
- Lesson 4 revised (chapter 7 renamed to Sub Spe's "a possible result"; new "Where the formula stops" section; predict-drill now grades stage, not scene). Lesson 5 built on the Seven of Swords as case study; the divergent cards are taught as named exceptions with their own ancestry.
- Teaching preference to honour permanently: when a system is taught, state its failure modes inside the same lesson, with the same prominence as its successes. He stress-tests claims with random spot-checks, so any overclaim will surface.
