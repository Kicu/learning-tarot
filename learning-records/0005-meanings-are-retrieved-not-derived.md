# At the table, meanings are retrieved, not derived — the systems are for encoding

After finishing the minor-arcana block (lessons 4–6), Mateusz stepped back and named the cost of the multi-source approach: every rule from Etteilla, Book T or Waite carries exceptions, and picking a card's meaning now involves "a lot of mental maths" — is this card straightforward from the formula, is it the picture, is it a court card. He proposed two alternatives: commit to a single source and list its every connection per card, or learn the meanings by heart.

The resolution extends [[0004-formula-is-an-ancestor-not-a-generator]] from one card to the whole learning strategy. No single source can serve as "the" source, because the RWS meanings are a historical composite — roughly half of Waite's printed lists match the Etteilla school (Revak), Book T supplies structure that ignores many pictures, and the pictures themselves add meanings neither text predicted. Any one source's per-card list contradicts the deck in hand somewhere (Waite's Hermit: "treason"; Book T's Seven of Swords: no thief). Choosing one source does not remove the exceptions, it hides them.

What practicing readers actually carry is memorized card-level meanings. The systems' real work happens at encoding time — they turn 78 arbitrary facts into mostly-predictable items plus a shortlist of named deviations — and at justification time, which the mission explicitly requires ("cite the visual evidence"). Reading time should use one procedure only: picture first, number as stakes, suit as register, courts by the three ordered questions. Source arbitration at the moment of reading is a category error the course accidentally taught.

## Evidence

He diagnosed the retrieval problem himself after honest use of the material across three lessons, distinguished it from a complaint about multi-source teaching ("my gripe is not with all the different sources"), and independently arrived at the strategy (deliberate memorization) that matches how expertise research says pattern recognition is actually built. This is the second time a course-level correction originated from his own stress-testing (first: [[0004-formula-is-an-ancestor-not-a-generator]]).

## Implications

- Course pivot: add a memorization track. Its backbone is a single master reference — one canonical line per minor card, anchored in what Smith's picture shows, with the stage-from-number and a small ancestry tag as the memory glue, not as competing rules.
- Storage strength needs spaced retrieval drills (blind draw from the physical deck → speak the meaning → check the sheet), repeated across sessions, interleaved with the remaining lesson arc rather than replacing it.
- The "why" is not abandoned — its role changes from derivation tool to mnemonic glue and justification. This matches the mission's load-bearing phrase better than the systems-first framing did: he should *know* the meaning and be *able* to explain why, not compute one from the other.
- Future lessons must keep the two moments separate: encoding (where sources and exceptions live) vs reading (one procedure, no arbitration).
