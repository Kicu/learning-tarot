# Tarot Resources

Curated, high-trust only. Tarot has an unusually bad signal-to-noise ratio: most of what is published repeats Court de Gébelin's 1781 fabrication as fact. Everything below is either a primary document, serious historical scholarship, or a practitioner who is honest about the difference.

## Knowledge — Primary documents

- [A. E. Waite, _The Pictorial Key to the Tarot_ (1911) — full text](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/pkt/index.htm)
  The companion book to the deck Mateusz owns, written by the man who directed the imagery. Free and complete. Use for: what Waite *said* each card means, his Celtic Cross instructions, and his own sniffy disclaimers about fortune-telling. Essential — but it is a primary source for Waite's intentions, **not** for history.
- [_The Pictorial Key to the Tarot_ — scans on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/A.EWaiteThePictorialKeyToTheTarot)
  Same text with the original plates, if the layout matters.
- [Antoine Court de Gébelin, _Le Monde primitif_ vol. VIII (1781) — context and translation](https://waitesmith.org/index.php/pre-history/antoine-court-de-gebelin/court-de-gebelin/)
  The document that invented the Egyptian origin myth. Use for: seeing exactly how thin the original claim was.

## Knowledge — History and scholarship

- [Michael Dummett, _The Game of Tarot: From Ferrara to Salt Lake City_ (1980)](https://www.weiserantiquarian.com/pages/books/72742/michael-dummett-with-the-assistance-of-sylvia-mann/the-game-of-tarot-from-ferrara-to-salt-lake-city)
  The foundational scholarly work; established the 15th-century Italian origin and demonstrated that no divinatory use is recorded before the 18th century. Out of print and expensive — read *about* it before buying. Use for: settling any historical dispute.
- [Tarot Heritage — "Michael Dummett: A Key Figure in Tarot History"](https://tarotheritage.com/michael-dummett-a-key-figure-in-tarot-history/)
  Accessible summary of what Dummett established and why it superseded everything before it. Use for: the short version of the scholarly consensus.
- [Trionfi.com — Origin of Tarot](http://trionfi.com/0/index2.php)
  Research archive on 15th-century trionfi and early playing cards: dated documents, deck lists, an iconography museum. Ugly site, excellent content. Use for: primary-document dates and early deck comparisons.
- [Franco Pratesi — playing-card research, translated](https://pratesitranslations.blogspot.com/)
  Archival researcher who found the 1440 Florence reference, pushing the earliest record back from Ferrara 1442. Use for: the current earliest-evidence question.
- [Andrea Vitali — iconographical essays on the trumps (Associazione Le Tarot)](http://trionfi.com/tarot-books)
  Card-by-card iconography grounded in period Italian sources. Use for: what a trump image meant to a 15th-century Italian, before any occult reading.
- [Mary K. Greer's Tarot Blog — Tarot History & Research](https://marykgreer.com/category/tarot-history-research/)
  Golden Dawn initiate, co-author of _Pamela Colman Smith: The Untold Story_, and unusually rigorous about sourcing. Use for: Golden Dawn correspondences, Smith's biography, and where a "traditional" meaning actually came from.
- [House of White — Waite-Smith Tarot Museum & Research Library](https://waitesmith.org/)
  Edition-by-edition documentation of the 1909 deck and its printings. Use for: which version of the deck an image comes from.
- [Hyperallergic — "The Unsung Woman Artist Behind Your Tarot Cards"](https://hyperallergic.com/the-unnamed-woman-artist-revealed-in-the-monogram-of-your-tarot-cards/)
  On Pamela Colman Smith's authorship and erasure. Use for: the Smith attribution question.
- [Wikipedia — Mamluk playing cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_playing_cards)
  The Topkapı pack and the cups/coins/swords/polo-sticks suit system Europe imported in the 1370s. Well-sourced overview; follow its citations for depth. Use for: where the four suit signs came from. (The World of Playing Cards page on this 404s — do not cite it.)

## Knowledge — Occult primary documents

- [Golden Dawn, _Book T — The Tarot_ (c. 1888–96), full text at hermetic.com](https://hermetic.com/gdlibrary/book-t)
  The order's internal tarot curriculum: suit–element assignments, card titles, astrological attributions. **A primary source for layer-2 doctrine, not for history** — cite it to show who asserted a correspondence and when. Use for: any "where does that meaning come from" question whose answer is "the Golden Dawn."

## Knowledge — Card images

- [Wikimedia Commons — Works by Pamela Colman Smith](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Works_by_Pamela_Colman_Smith)
  Public domain in the US and UK for the original 1909 deck (note: some modern recolourings are still under copyright). Files follow `RWS Tarot NN Name.jpg` for trumps and `Swords03.jpg` / `Cups07.jpg` for suit cards. Local copies live in `./assets/img/`.
- [Wikimedia Commons — Pierpont Morgan-Bergamo Visconti-Sforza Tarot](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Pierpont_Morgan-Bergamo_Visconti-Sforza_Tarot) · [Sacred-texts RWS copyright FAQ](https://sacred-texts.com/tarot/faq.htm)
  The c. 1450 hand-painted deck, and the licensing situation for RWS scans.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/tarot](https://reddit.com/r/tarot)
  Largest active community; explicitly covers history and theory alongside practice, not just "what does this mean for my ex." Use for: getting a reading critiqued, and comparing how experienced readers justify an interpretation.
- [Tarot History Forum](https://forum.tarothistory.com/)
  Small, serious, historically literate — where Dummett-adjacent researchers actually post. Use for: any question about dates, provenance, or early decks. Do not use for divination questions; wrong room.
- [Tarot Forum (tarotforum.net)](https://www.tarotforum.net/)
  Successor to the old Aeclectic community; large archive of card-by-card discussion threads. Use for: seeing many readers' takes on one card.

## Gaps

- **No trusted single volume on RWS symbolism yet.** Rachel Pollack's _Seventy-Eight Degrees of Wisdom_ and Waite's own _Pictorial Key_ are the usual recommendations; Pollack is insightful but interpretive rather than historical, and needs vetting before I put it on this list.
- **Smith's own sources are under-documented.** Her visual borrowings (Sola-Busca deck for several suit cards, theatre design, Yeats's circle) are asserted more often than demonstrated. Needs a real source before I teach it as fact.
